Liquidity Ratios

By Sahil Pekde · Measure short-term financial resilience using current, quick and cash ratios.

1. What liquidity ratios measure

Liquidity is the ability to meet obligations as they fall due. Ratios are screening tools; timing of receipts and payments matters as much as the closing balance sheet.

2. Three common ratios

RatioFormulaInterpretation
Current ratioCurrent assets ÷ Current liabilitiesBroad short-term coverage
Quick ratio(Current assets − inventory − prepayments) ÷ Current liabilitiesCoverage without less-liquid current assets
Cash ratioCash and cash equivalents ÷ Current liabilitiesImmediate cash coverage

3. Worked example

Assume current assets ₹90 lakh, including inventory ₹20 lakh, prepayments ₹10 lakh and cash ₹15 lakh. Current liabilities are ₹60 lakh.

1.50×Current ratio = 90/60
1.00×Quick ratio = (90−20−10)/60
0.25×Cash ratio = 15/60

Despite a current ratio above 1, cash alone covers only one-quarter of current liabilities. The company depends on collections or other financing.

4. Interpret carefully

A higher ratio is not automatically better: excessive inventory or overdue receivables can inflate current assets. Compare with historical performance, business model, industry peers and near-term payment schedules. A negative working-capital model can be normal in some cash-generative businesses.

CFO perspective: Request a 13-week cash forecast, receivables ageing and committed debt/lease payments before concluding that liquidity is adequate.
Try it yourself: Current assets ₹75 lakh; inventory ₹15 lakh; prepayments ₹5 lakh; current liabilities ₹50 lakh. Calculate current ratio (1.50×) and quick ratio (1.10×).
