Receivables and Payables Ageing

By Sahil Pekde · Use ageing schedules to evaluate collections, overdue balances and supplier obligations.

1. Why ageing matters

Ageing schedules group unpaid customer invoices and supplier bills by how long they have remained outstanding. They reveal collection risk, overdue obligations and operational bottlenecks. Define whether ageing is measured from invoice date or due date.

Receivables overdue by more than 30 days total ₹12 lakh (30% of the outstanding balance). This does not automatically equal bad debt; assess disputes, customer creditworthiness and expected credit losses under applicable accounting standards.

3. Payables ageing example

BucketAmount ₹ lakh
Not yet due15
1–30 days overdue7
31–60 days overdue3

₹8 lakh is more than 30 days overdue. Investigate supplier disputes, missed approvals and cash constraints. Prioritise statutory and critical obligations according to their legal and operational implications.

4. A practical collections workflow

  1. Reconcile customer subledger to the general ledger.
  2. Confirm invoice delivery, due dates and payment terms.
  3. Segment balances by amount, age, customer risk and dispute status.
  4. Assign collection owners and dated follow-up actions.
  5. Review expected credit loss/provisioning where applicable.
  6. Track actual collections against the weekly cash forecast.
CFO perspective: Monitor overdue value, DSO, top-customer concentration and disputed balances together. A single ageing percentage can hide a material exposure.
Try it yourself: A customer owes ₹5 lakh, of which ₹2 lakh is over 60 days overdue. What percentage of that customer balance is over 60 days overdue? Answer: 40%.

Worked business case: Invoice ageing for a B2B services provider

The company has ₹20 lakh of open customer invoices, with a material portion past agreed due dates.

Item / transactionAmount or entryMeaning
Not yet due₹8 lakh40%
1–30 days overdue₹5 lakh25%
31–60 days overdue₹4 lakh20%
Over 60 days overdue₹3 lakh15%
Financial interpretation: ₹12 lakh, or 60%, is overdue. The ₹3 lakh over 60 days deserves targeted collection action and an expected-credit-loss assessment as applicable. Ageing buckets should be based on due date when measuring overdue status.

Apply it yourself

Practice question: What is the overdue share if ₹9 lakh of ₹15 lakh invoices is past due?
Show worked answer

₹9 lakh ÷ ₹15 lakh = 60%. Segment by customer, dispute reason and collectability.

Think like a CFO

Assign owners to overdue balances; reconcile ageing to the general ledger and track collections against promises.
