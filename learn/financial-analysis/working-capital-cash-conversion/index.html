Working Capital and Cash Conversion Cycle

By Sahil Pekde · Calculate operating working capital and the cash conversion cycle.

1. Working capital connects profit to cash

Operating working capital generally reflects receivables and inventory less operating payables. The exact definition varies by business; exclude cash and interest-bearing debt when analysing operating working capital unless you explicitly define otherwise.

2. The cash conversion cycle

CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO

DIO is days inventory outstanding; DSO is days sales outstanding; DPO is days payables outstanding. For an annual approximation, use average balances and a consistent 365-day basis.

MeasureIllustrative formula
DSOAverage trade receivables ÷ credit revenue × 365
DIOAverage inventory ÷ cost of goods sold × 365
DPOAverage trade payables ÷ relevant credit purchases or cost of sales × 365 (state basis)

3. Worked example

Assume DIO = 35 days, DSO = 50 days and DPO = 40 days.

45 daysCash conversion cycle = 35 + 50 − 40

On this simplified basis, cash is tied up for about 45 days between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Shortening DSO to 42 days reduces CCC to 37 days, all else equal.

4. Improving working capital

  • Bill promptly and remove collection disputes.
  • Negotiate commercially sustainable payment terms.
  • Reduce slow-moving inventory and improve demand planning.
  • Forecast deposits, rent, taxes and other contract-based cash commitments.
  • Review operational causes, not just month-end ratios.
CFO perspective: Estimate the cash released by an improvement in DSO: daily credit revenue × reduction in days. Validate assumptions and seasonality before presenting this as guaranteed cash.
Try it yourself: DIO 20 days, DSO 55 days, DPO 30 days. Calculate CCC = 45 days. What happens if DSO falls by 10 days? CCC becomes 35 days.

Worked business case: Cash conversion cycle in a trading company

A distributor holds inventory, sells on credit and receives 45-day supplier terms.

Item / transactionAmount or entryMeaning
Days inventory outstanding55 daysAverage stock / COGS × 365
Days sales outstanding40 daysAverage receivables / credit sales × 365
Days payables outstanding45 daysAverage trade payables / COGS × 365
Cash conversion cycle50 days55 + 40 − 45
Financial interpretation: The company finances roughly 50 days between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Reducing DIO by 10 days and DSO by 5 days would reduce the cycle to 35 days, assuming DPO stays unchanged.

Apply it yourself

Practice question: DIO 60 days, DSO 35 days, DPO 50 days. What is CCC?
Show worked answer

60 + 35 − 50 = 45 days. Shorter is often better, provided customer service and supplier relationships are not harmed.

Think like a CFO

Improve inventory planning and collections before using expensive short-term borrowing to cover avoidable working-capital gaps.
