Horizontal and Vertical Analysis

By Sahil Pekde · Compare financial performance across periods and analyse each line as a percentage of a meaningful base.

1. Horizontal analysis: what changed?

Horizontal analysis compares the same financial statement line across periods. Use both absolute change and percentage change. If the base period is zero or negative, a standard growth percentage can be misleading or undefined.

Absolute change = Current period − Prior period
Percentage change = (Current − Prior) ÷ |Prior| × 100, where meaningful

2. Example: a growing business

Line itemFY25 ₹ lakhFY26 ₹ lakhChangeChange %
Revenue100120+20+20%
Cost of services6078+18+30%
Operating expenses2527+2+8%
Operating profit151500%

Revenue grew 20%, but cost of services rose 30%. Operating profit remained ₹15 lakh: growth did not improve operating profit.

3. Vertical analysis: what is the composition?

For a P&L, express costs and profit as a percentage of revenue. For a balance sheet, a common base is total assets. Ensure consistent definitions and periods.

MetricFY25FY26
Cost of services / Revenue60.0%65.0%
Operating expenses / Revenue25.0%22.5%
Operating profit margin15.0%12.5%

4. Interpreting the combination

The cost-of-services ratio deteriorated by 5 percentage points. The operating-expense ratio improved by 2.5 points, partly offsetting the pressure. Investigate pricing, supplier rates, utilisation and service mix before drawing conclusions.

CFO perspective: Always distinguish percentage growth from percentage-point changes. A 15% margin falling to 12.5% is a 2.5 percentage-point decline, not a 2.5% decline.
Try it yourself: If revenue rises from ₹80 lakh to ₹100 lakh and gross profit rises from ₹32 lakh to ₹35 lakh, calculate revenue growth (25%) and gross margin movement (40% to 35%).

Lesson review

Explain the main metric in your own words. Identify one management decision that the analysis could support.

Worked business case: Analyse a product company over two years

A consumer-goods business grows revenue while its production and distribution costs rise faster.

Item / transactionAmount or entryMeaning
Revenue₹80 lakh → ₹100 lakh+25.0%
COGS₹48 lakh → ₹65 lakh+35.4%
Gross profit₹32 lakh → ₹35 lakh+9.4%
Gross margin40% → 35%−5 percentage points
Financial interpretation: Horizontal analysis shows sales growth of 25%. Vertical analysis shows gross margin deteriorating from 40% to 35%. Investigate input-cost inflation, discounts, product mix, wastage and selling prices.

Apply it yourself

Practice question: If operating expenses rise from ₹12 lakh to ₹15 lakh, what is their growth rate?
Show worked answer

(₹15 lakh − ₹12 lakh) ÷ ₹12 lakh = 25%. Compare it with revenue growth and operating margin, not in isolation.

Think like a CFO

Separate price, volume, mix and cost inflation. A higher revenue figure is not automatically stronger performance.
