Trend Analysis and Management Insights

By Sahil Pekde · Turn financial trends into management insights, forecasts and corrective actions.

1. Move from numbers to decisions

Trend analysis compares performance over several periods and asks what is structural, seasonal, one-off or caused by accounting changes. A useful management report explains why a metric moved, what happens next and who owns the response.

2. Three-year business case

MetricFY24FY25FY26
Revenue (₹ lakh)100120150
EBITDA (₹ lakh)202122.5
EBITDA margin20.0%17.5%15.0%
DSO (days)354358

Revenue increased 50% over two years, but EBITDA margin declined 5 percentage points and DSO increased 23 days. Sales growth is not translating proportionately into earnings or cash conversion.

3. Diagnostic questions

  1. Is growth coming from price, volume, customer mix or acquisitions?
  2. Which direct costs rose faster than revenue?
  3. Are overheads fixed, semi-variable or discretionary?
  4. Is DSO rising due to slower collections, contract terms or billing errors?
  5. Are one-off items, accounting changes or seasonality distorting the comparison?

4. Convert insight into an action plan

FindingManagement actionOwner / metric
Margin deteriorationReview pricing, supplier contracts and service mixCommercial + Finance / EBITDA margin
DSO risingResolve billing disputes and implement weekly collections reviewAR lead / DSO and overdue value
Revenue concentrationStress-test forecast against top-customer churnFP&A / concentration and downside case

5. Present an executive summary

Observation: Revenue grew, but margin and collections deteriorated. Business implication: Expansion may be consuming cash and weakening returns. Action: Build a margin bridge and 13-week cash forecast, assign owners, and review weekly until the trend stabilises.

CFO perspective: A CFO-quality dashboard should show actual, budget, prior period, trend, risk threshold, owner and action—not just a colourful chart.
Try it yourself: If EBITDA rises from ₹20 lakh to ₹22.5 lakh while revenue rises from ₹100 lakh to ₹150 lakh, explain why EBITDA margin falls from 20% to 15%.
