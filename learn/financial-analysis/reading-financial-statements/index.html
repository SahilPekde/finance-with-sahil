How to Read and Validate Financial Statements

By Sahil Pekde · Understand what financial statements reveal and verify the reliability of their underlying numbers.

1. Begin with the quality of the numbers

Financial analysis starts before ratios. Confirm that the reporting period is complete, material journals are approved, bank and control accounts are reconciled, and the financial statements follow consistent accounting policies. A sophisticated ratio calculated from unreliable books is still unreliable.

2. What each statement answers

StatementPrimary questionKey review
Profit & LossDid the business earn a profit?Revenue recognition, expenses, margins and one-offs
Balance SheetWhat does the business own and owe at the reporting date?Liquidity, debt, receivables and provisions
Cash FlowWhere did cash come from and where did it go?Operating cash flow, investment and financing movements

3. A practical quality-control sequence

  1. Confirm the reporting period, entity and currency.
  2. Check bank reconciliations and customer/vendor subledgers.
  3. Review cut-off, accruals, prepayments, depreciation and provisions.
  4. Reconcile opening and closing equity and cash movements.
  5. Investigate unusual balances, manual journals and related-party items.
  6. Document adjustments and unresolved exceptions before presenting KPIs.

4. Mini case: profit without cash

A company records ₹20 lakh in sales and ₹15 lakh in expenses, reporting ₹5 lakh profit. If ₹12 lakh of sales remain uncollected and expenses were largely paid, cash may be tight despite reported profit. Profit is measured on an accrual basis; cash flow reflects actual cash movements and other adjustments.

CFO perspective: Ask: Which balances are supported by evidence? Which assumptions are material? Which cash commitments fall due before customers pay?
Try it yourself: A company has ₹10 lakh of revenue booked against a customer advance for services not yet delivered. Identify the possible revenue cut-off error and its effect on profit and liabilities.
