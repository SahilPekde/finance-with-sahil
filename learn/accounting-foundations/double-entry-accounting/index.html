Accounting Principles and Double-Entry Bookkeeping

By Sahil Pekde · Clear concepts, journal entries, business examples and CFO insights.

1. What is accounting?

Accounting is the process of recording, classifying and reporting business transactions. It helps explain profitability, financial position and liquidity. Transactions can involve selling products, delivering services, paying employees, buying equipment or raising funds.

2. The accounting equation

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Assets are resources controlled by a business; liabilities are present obligations; equity is the residual interest. For example, ₹10 lakh of assets financed by ₹4 lakh borrowings and ₹6 lakh equity satisfy the equation.

3. Debit and credit rules

Every journal entry has equal total debits and credits. Debit and credit are accounting directions—not synonyms for money received and paid.

Account typeIncreaseDecrease
AssetsDebitCredit
ExpensesDebitCredit
LiabilitiesCreditDebit
EquityCreditDebit
RevenueCreditDebit

Recognise revenue when the applicable revenue-recognition criteria are satisfied, not simply when money arrives. Similarly, record expenses in the appropriate accounting period.

Worked business case: Trading company: April transactions

ABC Traders starts operations with ₹5,00,000 capital, buys goods on credit, sells part of its inventory and pays salaries. Ignore GST; use perpetual inventory.

Item / transactionAmount or entryMeaning
Owner introduces capitalBank Dr ₹5,00,000Capital Cr ₹5,00,000
Goods purchased on creditInventory Dr ₹1,20,000Trade Payables Cr ₹1,20,000
Credit saleTrade Receivables Dr ₹80,000Sales Cr ₹80,000
Cost of goods soldCOGS Dr ₹50,000Inventory Cr ₹50,000
Customer collectionBank Dr ₹30,000Receivables Cr ₹30,000
Salary paymentSalary Expense Dr ₹20,000Bank Cr ₹20,000
Financial interpretation: Sales ₹80,000 less COGS ₹50,000 and salary ₹20,000 gives ₹10,000 profit. Bank closes at ₹5,10,000; receivables ₹50,000; inventory ₹70,000; payables ₹1,20,000. Assets ₹6,30,000 equal liabilities ₹1,20,000 plus equity ₹5,10,000.

Apply it yourself

Practice question: A customer pays ₹60,000 today for services to be delivered next month. What entry is needed on receipt?
Show worked answer

Debit Bank ₹60,000; credit Contract Liability / Unearned Revenue ₹60,000. Recognise revenue as the service obligation is fulfilled.

Think like a CFO

A balanced entry is necessary but not sufficient: test cut-off, correct account classification, supporting invoices and cash collections.
