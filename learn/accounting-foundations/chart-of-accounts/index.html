Chart of Accounts and Account Classification

By Sahil Pekde · Design a useful chart of accounts and avoid misleading classifications.

1. What is a chart of accounts?

A chart of accounts (COA) is the controlled list of ledger accounts used to classify transactions. It is the bridge between daily bookkeeping and management reporting. A good COA separates what the business owns, owes, earns and spends.

For example, a ₹50,000 security deposit paid to a landlord is generally a recoverable asset, not rent expense. Putting it in rent expense understates assets and profit.

4. Map accounts to reporting

Each ledger should map to a balance-sheet or P&L line, a department/cost centre where useful, and an owner responsible for reconciliation. Do not create a separate GL for every property if an analytical dimension can handle the property split more cleanly.

CFO perspective: A poor COA can make profitable units look loss-making, conceal overdue liabilities and produce unreliable KPIs. Design the COA around decision-useful reporting, not just voucher entry.

5. Common classification errors

  • Booking refundable security deposits as expenses.
  • Booking owner/director loans as revenue.
  • Mixing principal repayments with interest expense.
  • Recording fixed asset purchases as repairs without evaluating capitalisation.
  • Using a generic “miscellaneous” ledger for material transactions.
Practice: A company pays ₹1,20,000 refundable deposit and ₹40,000 monthly rent. Classify both: the deposit is normally an asset; the rent is an expense for the relevant period.
