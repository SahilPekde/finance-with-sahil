Journal Entries, Ledgers and Reconciliations

By Sahil Pekde · Follow source documents into entries, ledgers and reconciliations.

1. Source document → journal → ledger

Start with evidence: an invoice, agreement, bank advice or approved expense claim. A journal entry captures debit and credit effects. The general ledger then accumulates transactions by account so balances can be checked.

Vendor invoice → Approval → Journal entry → Vendor ledger → Payment → Bank reconciliation

2. Three practical journal entries

EventDebitCredit
Invoice for utilities ₹12,000Utilities expense ₹12,000Trade payable ₹12,000
Pay supplier ₹12,000Trade payable ₹12,000Bank ₹12,000
Receive customer advance ₹60,000Bank ₹60,000Contract liability / customer advance ₹60,000

Assume the utility invoice is for the current period and the advance relates to services not yet provided. An advance is not automatically revenue.

3. Ledger posting and running balance

Suppose the bank opens at ₹1,00,000, receives ₹60,000 customer advance and pays ₹12,000 to a vendor. Closing bank balance is ₹1,48,000.

Bank movementDebit ₹Credit ₹Balance ₹
Opening balance1,00,000—1,00,000
Customer advance60,000—1,60,000
Vendor payment—12,0001,48,000

4. Three essential reconciliations

  • Bank: Compare bank statement with ledger; identify unpresented cheques, deposits in transit and bank charges.
  • Trade receivables/payables: Agree subledger totals with control GLs; investigate unapplied receipts and debit balances.
  • Tax: Reconcile GST/TDS ledgers to returns, challans and supporting registers as applicable.

5. Reconciliation example

Bank statement balance ₹1,52,000. Less an issued cheque not yet presented ₹4,000 gives adjusted bank balance ₹1,48,000, matching the ledger. Check the cheque is genuine and still outstanding.

CFO perspective: Unreconciled accounts are not just accounting problems; they can signal cash leakage, duplicate payments, overstated receivables or weak controls.
Practice: Ledger bank balance ₹2,40,000; bank statement ₹2,55,000; unpresented cheques ₹15,000. Reconciled statement balance = ₹2,40,000.
