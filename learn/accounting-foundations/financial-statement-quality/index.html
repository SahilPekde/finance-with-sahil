Financial Statement Accuracy and Quality Checks

By Sahil Pekde · Detect errors before management relies on the numbers.

1. Accuracy begins before ratios

A financial statement may look polished and still be wrong. First validate completeness, cut-off, classification, measurement and presentation. Only then use it for ratio analysis or forecasting.

2. Seven quality checks

CheckWhat to verifyRisk if missed
CompletenessAll invoices, accruals and bank accounts capturedUnderstated costs or liabilities
Cut-offTransactions belong to correct periodMisleading monthly profit
ClassificationDeposit vs expense; loan vs revenueWrong KPIs and ratios
ExistenceReceivables, assets and cash supportedOverstated assets
ValuationBad debts, depreciation and provisions consideredOverstated earnings
ReconciliationSubledgers match control accountsHidden mismatches
PresentationRelated parties and current/non-current items correctPoor decisions or disclosure risk

3. Mini diagnostic case

Management reports revenue ₹50 lakh, EBITDA ₹8 lakh and bank ₹3 lakh. You discover a ₹4 lakh customer advance booked as revenue even though services begin next month. Assuming no related current-period cost, corrected revenue is ₹46 lakh and EBITDA ₹4 lakh; customer advance liability increases by ₹4 lakh. Cash remains ₹3 lakh.

4. How to investigate a suspicious number

  1. Compare current month with budget, previous month and same month last year.
  2. Drill down to the GL and underlying source documents.
  3. Check journal dates, approvals and accounting policy.
  4. Quantify the correction and the effect on KPIs.
  5. Record the issue, owner, corrective action and prevention control.
CFO perspective: A finance leader should not present a ratio without confidence in its numerator, denominator and accounting policy. Document assumptions and material unresolved issues.
Practice: Reported receivables ₹15 lakh include a duplicate invoice of ₹2 lakh. Corrected receivables are ₹13 lakh; investigate the corresponding revenue/tax impact before posting.
