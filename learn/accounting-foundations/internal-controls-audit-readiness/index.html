Internal Controls and Audit Readiness

By Sahil Pekde · Protect assets, strengthen approvals and prepare for audit.

1. What is an internal control?

An internal control is a policy or activity designed to reduce the risk of error, fraud, non-compliance or unreliable reporting. Controls may prevent a problem, detect it after occurrence, or correct it.

2. A practical finance control matrix

3. Segregation of duties

Ideally, one person should not create a vendor, approve an invoice, release a payment and reconcile the bank. Smaller teams may need compensating controls such as independent review by a manager, restricted system rights and periodic exception reports.

4. Audit-ready documentation

  • Maintain an approved chart of accounts and accounting policies.
  • Retain invoices, contracts, bank evidence and tax support.
  • Keep reconciliations with preparer/reviewer sign-offs.
  • Document estimates, judgements and material manual entries.
  • Track audit observations to owners and closure dates.

5. Audit readiness versus audit opinion

Being organised for audit does not itself guarantee an unmodified audit opinion. Auditors assess evidence and material misstatements under applicable standards. Management remains responsible for preparing reliable financial statements and maintaining appropriate controls.

CFO perspective: Strong controls improve cash protection, reporting speed, lender confidence and management decision-making—not merely audit compliance.
Practice: A clerk can create vendors and initiate payments. Propose two safeguards: independent vendor approval and separate bank payment authorisation, with periodic vendor-master review.
