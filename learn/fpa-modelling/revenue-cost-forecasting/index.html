Revenue and Cost Forecasting

By Sahil Pekde · Translate pipeline, order volume, pricing, headcount and contracts into forecasts.

Learning objective: Translate pipeline, order volume, pricing, headcount and contracts into forecasts. Apply the method to a realistic business situation and explain the management decision it supports.

1. Forecast the expected outcome

Forecasting estimates what is now likely to happen, incorporating new information. A good forecast separates contracted business, probable opportunities and speculative upside. It should be updated when drivers change rather than mechanically extrapolating the latest month.

Distinguish a signed lease or order from a prospect. Use documented assumptions for probability, start date, churn, pricing and collection timing.

2. Revenue build-up

For recurring revenue, begin with opening run-rate, add new starts and price increases, then subtract churn and vacancies. For project revenue, use deliverables, timing and applicable recognition criteria. Avoid confusing billed amounts, recognised revenue and cash collected.

Illustrative bridge: opening monthly recurring revenue ₹30 lakh + new contracts ₹4 lakh − vacancies ₹2 lakh + price increases ₹1 lakh = closing run-rate ₹33 lakh.

3. Cost forecasting

Forecast payroll from employees × cost per employee and planned joining dates. Forecast direct costs using unit costs and activity volumes. Model fixed commitments from lease agreements, software contracts, interest schedules and known renewals.

If monthly revenue is ₹33 lakh and variable direct costs are 30% of revenue, variable costs are ₹9.90 lakh. Add fixed operating costs of ₹12 lakh: operating contribution after these costs is ₹11.10 lakh, before depreciation, interest and tax.

4. Timing, sensitivity and reconciliation

Show the monthly ramp, not just an annual total. Reconcile opening run-rate to closing run-rate and distinguish one-off items. Run sensitivity on order volume, price, volume and cost inflation.

CFO perspective: Explain the three most important forecast drivers, who owns them, and how a miss affects liquidity and covenants.

Formula and worked application

Closing recurring revenue run-rate = Opening run-rate + New starts + Price increases − Churn and vacancies
Try it yourself: Opening revenue ₹50 lakh, new starts ₹8 lakh, churn ₹5 lakh, price increases ₹2 lakh: closing run-rate = ₹55 lakh.
