Budgeting Fundamentals

By Sahil Pekde · Build an operating budget from commercial drivers, costs and cash requirements.

Learning objective: Build an operating budget from commercial drivers, costs and cash requirements. Apply the method to a realistic business situation and explain the management decision it supports.

1. What a budget actually does

A budget is a quantified operating plan for a defined period. It translates strategic objectives into revenue, operating costs, investment, financing and cash requirements. Unlike a forecast, which updates the expected outcome, an approved budget is normally a performance baseline.

A budget should specify its assumptions, owners, period, version and approval status. Without these, budget-versus-actual comparisons can become misleading.

2. Start with drivers, not last year plus 10%

Build revenue from units × price, or occupied properties × average rent, or billable hours × rate. Build costs from headcount, salary rates, contracts, volume and known commitments. Separate fixed, variable and step-fixed costs.

A driver-based model allows management to ask what happens if occupancy, prices, collections or headcount change.

3. Worked example: a rental-services business

Assume 20 units, 90% expected average occupancy and monthly rental revenue of ₹1.50 lakh per occupied unit. Expected monthly revenue = 20 × 90% × ₹1.50 lakh = ₹27.00 lakh. Assume property-related direct costs of ₹12.00 lakh and overhead of ₹8.00 lakh.

Monthly contribution after direct costs = ₹15.00 lakh; illustrative operating profit before depreciation, interest and tax = ₹7.00 lakh. This is not necessarily cash generated: timing of rent receipts, deposits, capital expenditure and payments matters.

4. Budget governance

Ask department owners to sign off on assumptions. Reconcile the operating budget to headcount, planned capex, debt service and cash forecasts. Maintain an approved baseline and record changes separately.

CFO perspective: Present the base budget with a downside case, minimum liquidity threshold and actions triggered by underperformance.

Formula and worked application

Budget revenue = Operating units × Utilisation or occupancy × Average price
Try it yourself: If 24 units have 75% occupancy and earn ₹1.20 lakh each per month, budget monthly revenue is ₹21.60 lakh.
