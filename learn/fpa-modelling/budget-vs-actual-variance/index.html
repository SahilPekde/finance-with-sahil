Budget vs Actual Variance Analysis

By Sahil Pekde · Explain performance gaps using price, volume, mix, timing and cost drivers.

Learning objective: Explain performance gaps using price, volume, mix, timing and cost drivers. Apply the method to a realistic business situation and explain the management decision it supports.

1. A variance is a question, not an explanation

A variance is actual performance minus a comparison baseline. A positive revenue variance is generally favourable, while a positive expense variance is generally unfavourable, but always label the sign convention. Distinguish controllable operating drivers from accounting timing and one-off events.

Do not report only “revenue below budget by ₹5 lakh.” Explain whether the gap came from occupancy, delayed start dates, pricing or collections versus recognition.

2. Worked example: volume and price

Budget: 100 units sold at ₹10,000 = ₹10.00 lakh. Actual: 90 units sold at ₹11,000 = ₹9.90 lakh. Total revenue variance = −₹0.10 lakh (unfavourable).

At budget price, volume variance = (90 − 100) × ₹10,000 = −₹1.00 lakh. At actual volume, price variance = (₹11,000 − ₹10,000) × 90 = +₹0.90 lakh. Combined = −₹0.10 lakh.

3. Costs and profitability bridge

Separate volume-linked costs from fixed costs. Compare gross margin, operating expenses and EBITDA or operating profit. Reconcile the bridge to the general ledger and approved budget version.

Example: actual maintenance is ₹2 lakh above budget because an emergency repair was required. Management needs to know whether this is one-off, recurring or avoidable, not merely that it is “unfavourable.”

4. Turn variance into action

Use an owner, cause, financial impact, corrective action and deadline. Escalate structural misses early and avoid reclassifying expenses just to improve the apparent result.

CFO perspective: Highlight forecast impact and liquidity consequences; a temporary accounting variance may not require the same action as a persistent unit-economics problem.

Formula and worked application

Revenue variance = Actual revenue − Budget revenue = Volume effect + Price effect (under the stated bridge method)
Try it yourself: Budget 200 units × ₹500; actual 180 × ₹550. Revenue variance = −₹1,000; volume effect −₹10,000 and price effect +₹9,000.
